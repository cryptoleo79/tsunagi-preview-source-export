# TSUNAGI Treasury

### Transparent On-Chain Funding for Independent Cardano Infrastructure

---

## 1. Why a Treasury Is Needed

Independent infrastructure development requires sustainable funding. TSUNAGI is developed outside institutional funding structures — no Catalyst proposals, no governance grants, no venture capital. The project depends on transparent, voluntary support from the community it serves.

Sustained development requires resources for:

- **Infrastructure** — Servers, network bandwidth, and storage for validation nodes on preview and mainnet networks.
- **Testing** — Extended soak runs, cross-network validation, and snapshot integrity verification require dedicated compute capacity.
- **Research** — Protocol specification analysis, cross-chain experimentation, and alternative storage engine evaluation require engineering time that cannot be deferred indefinitely.
- **Engineering time** — The deterministic ledger pipeline, shadow verification system, observability stack, and persistent storage engine represent thousands of hours of focused engineering work. Continued development, maintenance, and mainnet validation require ongoing commitment.
- **Long-term maintenance** — A node implementation is not a one-time deliverable. Protocol upgrades, hard fork compatibility, security patches, and operational tooling require sustained attention across the lifetime of the Cardano network.

Without sustainable funding, independent implementations either stall at the proof-of-concept stage or depend on the goodwill of a single developer working without compensation. Neither outcome serves the ecosystem's long-term resilience.

---

## 2. Independent Engineering

TSUNAGI began as an independent research and engineering effort. The project has been built without institutional grants, governance funding, or venture capital — sustained entirely through individual commitment and the conviction that Cardano benefits from implementation diversity, deterministic infrastructure, and operator-first design.

This independence is deliberate. Development priorities are driven by engineering judgment and operational reality, not by proposal cycles, funding committees, or external roadmap requirements. The architecture reflects choices made for technical merit — deterministic execution, explicit memory management, embedded observability — rather than choices shaped by what is fundable.

The work so far — a deterministic ledger pipeline, shadow verification, LMDB persistence, four observability subsystems, and continuous preview-network validation — represents thousands of hours of focused engineering. Sustaining this level of effort requires the project to develop a funding model that matches its independence: transparent, community-aligned, and free of institutional dependencies.

Community support matters because independent infrastructure work must become sustainable to remain independent. The treasury model described below is designed to achieve that sustainability while preserving the engineering autonomy that defines the project.

## 3. The TSUNAGI Funding Model

The TSUNAGI treasury operates as an on-chain funding mechanism aligned with Cardano's staking model.

The concept is straightforward:

- Contributions are sent to the **TSUNAGI treasury wallet** — a standard Cardano address dedicated to project funding.
- The treasury wallet **delegates to the TSUNAGI stake pool**, ensuring that contributed ADA is not idle but actively participating in the network.
- Delegated treasury stake becomes **productive**: it earns staking rewards that compound over time, creating a self-reinforcing funding mechanism.
- Staking rewards generated by the treasury delegation **support continued node development**, infrastructure costs, and research activities.
- All treasury activity is **publicly visible on-chain**. Any community member can verify the treasury wallet's balance, delegation status, and transaction history using standard Cardano blockchain explorers.

This model creates alignment between the project and the ecosystem: the treasury's value grows when the network succeeds, and the network benefits from the infrastructure diversity that the treasury funds.

## 4. Transparency Principles

TSUNAGI's treasury operates under the following transparency commitments:

- **Treasury wallet address** — Published and verifiable on-chain. All inflows and outflows are publicly auditable.
- **Stake pool identity** — Pool ticker and pool ID published, enabling delegators to verify that the treasury is delegated as stated.
- **Support address** — A dedicated ADA handle or address for community contributions, distinct from personal wallets.
- **Public funding explanation** — This document serves as the canonical explanation of the treasury model, its purpose, and its operational principles.
- **Separation of treasury and personal wallets** — The treasury wallet is a distinct address used exclusively for project funding. Personal funds are not commingled with treasury funds.

These commitments exist because transparency is not optional for a project that asks the community for support. Every ADA contributed to the treasury is visible, trackable, and accounted for.

---

## 5. Why This Model Matters

The treasury model addresses a structural challenge in blockchain infrastructure funding:

- **Self-sustaining infrastructure funding.** Rather than depending on periodic grant applications or one-time donations, the treasury generates ongoing staking rewards that support continued development. The funding mechanism is embedded in the same network the project serves.
- **Reduced dependence on grants.** Grant-funded development is subject to governance cycles, proposal competition, and political dynamics. Independent treasury funding allows development priorities to be set by engineering requirements rather than funding timelines.
- **Alignment with Cardano staking model.** The treasury participates in the network's consensus mechanism through delegation, contributing to decentralization rather than extracting value from it. Treasury stake strengthens the pool it delegates to, which in turn strengthens the network.
- **Long-term ecosystem resilience.** A sustainably funded independent implementation provides lasting infrastructure diversity. The treasury model ensures that this diversity does not depend on a single funding event or a single benefactor's continued interest.
